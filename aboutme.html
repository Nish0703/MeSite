    Knowing when a dataset has real groups in it
    Hanyang University, Seoul, South Korea · Research intern with Dr. Frank Chung Hoon Rhee · May to Jul 2025
    88.8% recovery vs 86.2% BIC, 83.8% AICscales to 50,000 records

    The question

    When you split data into groups, how many of them are real and how many are just noise? Pick too many and you chase patterns that aren't there.

    What I did

    Developed KL-Halt, a stopping rule for hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Models, built on forward KL divergence and a parametric bootstrap. I tested it on 4 datasets across 20 runs.

    The result

    It found the true structure 88.8% of the time, against 86.2% for BIC and 83.8% for AIC, and got 100% on the 2- and 3-cluster sets. Work grows roughly in line with data size. A paper is in preparation.
    why it matters outside the lab

    It's the same question behind customer segmentation: how many distinct user groups are there, and when should you stop slicing? Getting that wrong means building for segments that don't exist.

    Choosing which health signal is worth building a wearable around
    SRHU, Dehradun · Research intern with Prof. Neel Mani · May to Jul 2026
    23 biomarkers, 6 paperstop pick: salivary CRP, 7/10

    The question

    Saliva, sweat and breath can all carry health signals. Which ones are actually ready to power a wearable, and which just look promising in a paper?

    What I did

    Built a structured dataset of 23 biomarkers from 6 papers (3 of them reviews). It showed that only 1 of 23 reported diagnostic accuracy and only 6 had wearable sensors validated on people. Then I scored each biomarker on a 10-point rubric weighing evidence against wearable feasibility.

    The result

    A ranked shortlist with salivary CRP on top at 7/10, plus a clear map of where the evidence is thin.
    why it matters outside the lab

    This is a prioritisation exercise: gather scattered evidence, expose the gaps, score the options on a clear rubric, and recommend one. It's the same method as a market-entry or feature-prioritisation call.
